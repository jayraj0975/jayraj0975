# Market

Research date: 2026-09-27. Every statement below carries one label:

- **FACT**: stated by a primary or reputable secondary source, linked.
- **OBSERVATION**: something we saw directly (a product page, a spec, a repository).
- **ASSUMPTION**: our belief, not yet tested. Each one is a thing to go and check.
- **SCENARIO**: an explicit "if X then Y" calculation. Not a prediction.
- **FORECAST**: none in this document. We do not have the data to make one honestly.

## 1. The question this document answers

Is there a real, paid, recurring problem in establishing what AI-built software contains, where it came from and what could make it hard to finance, acquire, insure or operate? And if so, who pays for it?

**What this means (OBSERVATION):** the "why now" is not regulation (row 9 kills that argument). It is (a) a settled human-authorship rule that makes *which* code has human authorship a real diligence question, (b) indemnities that depend on facts a buyer cannot see, (c) buyers now asking the question explicitly, and (d) for the first time, machine-readable evidence exists to answer it, but only if someone collects it while it still exists.

**ASSUMPTION A1:** A seller preparing for an exit will pay $1,000-$3,000 for a credible readiness dossier they can run themselves before paying a $5k-$30k consultant or facing a buyer's Black Duck audit. *Test:* 20 seller conversations via M&A advisors; pre-sell 5 at list price.

**ASSUMPTION A2:** Small and mid-size acquirers (SaaS aggregators, search funds, micro-PE) run more deals than they can afford full audits for, and will pay for a buyer-side request workflow. *Test:* 10 conversations with aggregators; measure deals/year and current DD spend per deal.

**ASSUMPTION A3:** Once a company has a baseline dossier, continuous monitoring during a 3-12 month process (and during post-close integration) is worth a monthly fee. *Test:* conversion rate from one-time dossier to monthly plan among the first 20 paying sellers.

## 4. Market size: what we can and cannot say

We will not publish a TAM number. The data needed (number of transactions in which software code is diligenced, and spend per transaction) is not public. Here is what is known and how a size would be built.

|---|---|---|---|

| Software M&A deal value, 2025 | $362bn cumulative | FACT | [Silverpeak Q4 2025](https://www.silverpeakib.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Software-MA-report-Q4-2025.pdf) |

| Black Duck audits per year | ~1,000 codebases, hundreds of transactions | FACT | Black Duck white paper |
| Share of transactions where code is diligenced | unknown | UNKNOWN | none |

**SCENARIO S1 (not a forecast):** *If* N software-heavy transactions per year involve some form of code diligence, and a seller-side dossier sells at $1,500 with a buyer-side request at $750, then revenue per fully penetrated transaction is ~$2,250 before monitoring. At N = 5,000, full penetration is ~$11M/year; 10% penetration is ~$1.1M/year. The one-time transaction wedge alone does not make a venture-scale company. Venture scale requires the recurring layer (continuous custody for companies that expect to raise or sell within 24 months, plus post-close monitoring by acquirers and PE portfolios). That is **ASSUMPTION A3**, and it is the single most important thing to test.

## 5. Buyer map

| Buyer | Pain | Frequency | Pays today for | Our honest read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Founder preparing an exit (sell-side) | Surprises in buyer DD cut price or kill the deal | Once per process; process lasts months | Sell-side audits, consultants, lawyers | Strongest acute pain; self-serve possible; episodic |
| Small/mid acquirer (aggregator, search fund, micro-PE) | Cannot afford $20k audit per target; targets won't share code | Several deals/year | Consultants, gut feel | Good recurring buyer if the request workflow works |
| PE portfolio ops / corp dev at larger acquirer | 400-repo targets, one week, need triage | Per deal + post-close integration | Black Duck audits, CAST, Sema, consultancies | High value, sales-led; not our first market |
| Startup/M&A counsel | Needs facts for IP schedules and chain of title | Per deal | Their own time | Channel, not buyer |
| Series A founder | VCs rarely scan code at Series A (ASSUMPTION) | Rare | Little | Weak pain; demoted |
| CTO inheriting an AI-built codebase | Maintainability and security, less provenance | Once | Code audits | Secondary; served by the same dossier |
| R&W / tech E&O / AI insurers | Underwriting input on AI-code exposure | Per policy | Questionnaires | Long-term channel; AIUC exists for agents, not code provenance |

## 6. What would change our mind

- If sellers will not pay before a buyer asks (A1 fails), the company becomes buyer-led and sales-heavy. See WHY_NOT.md.
- If GitHub ships a neutral, cross-forge, signed diligence export, the self-serve wedge collapses to "use GitHub". See COMPETITORS.md.
- If acquirers keep relying on human-led audits and never accept a self-produced dossier (even a reproducible one), the network effect never starts.
